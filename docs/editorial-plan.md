# Little Home Ideas: editorial plan

Audience: U.S. renters and homeowners with modest spaces, budgets, and time. Voice: American English, concrete instructions, honest limitations. Positioning: help readers measure, decide, and plan before buying. Avoid generic “50 ideas” posts with no actionable detail.

## What the initial search check established

A spot check on October 13, 2026 covered no-closet entryways, storage around sink pipes, rental patio layouts, and window-condensation checklists. The results show existing competing guides and commercial articles. These are competitive topics, not proven low-difficulty keywords. No search-volume, ranking-difficulty, CPC, or traffic numbers were obtained or invented. This is a topic hypothesis with a small search sample, not a completed 30-keyword competition study.

Competitors are observations, not factual sources for our guides. Differentiate through original annotated diagrams, fit checks, maintenance access, clear restrictions, and helpful local tools. Before commissioning a new topic, inspect its U.S. search results, identify the intent and missing useful detail, and decide whether we can contribute something new.

## Publishing pace

Start with two reviewed articles per week for four weeks; increase toward four only when research, image, and review capacity supports it. No automatic publishing quota. Schedule around actual completion. October–November priorities: entry storage, indoor organization, patio winter storage where seasonally appropriate, and reversible indoor updates. Planting guides should link to local season and extension advice rather than assuming a nationwide spring date.

For each cluster, develop a useful hub plus focused supporting guides. Link from the hub to each genuinely related guide, from supporting guides back to the hub, and between guides only where the reader's next question warrants it. Current category pages are automatically generated hubs; add richer hub copy when enough specific content exists.

## Quality gate

1. One clear reader task and a specific reason the page adds value.
2. Primary sources for safety, technical, scientific, and manufacturer-dependent statements.
3. Original steps, examples, diagrams, and trade-offs; no copied listicle structure or fake firsthand tests.
4. No invented prices, performance numbers, search volumes, or product rankings.
5. American English; descriptive title; unique description; source attribution near technical claims.
6. Relevant internal links, useful image alt text, image provenance, and an appropriate safety boundary.
7. Re-read for contradictions, unclear assumptions, and claims that exceed evidence.
8. Set `reviewed: true` and `draft: false` only after review; CI and generated-site checks must pass.

Review higher-risk guides when source instructions change and at least every six months; review planning-only articles annually. Change `updated` only for a substantive review or edit. Never claim a guide has been tested unless a documented actual test took place.
