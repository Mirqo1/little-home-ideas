An empty strip beside the vanity can look like the perfect home for a slim cart. But the bathroom door may sweep across it, the bottom drawer may open into it, or the shelves may face a wall so closely that you cannot lift out a bottle.

Before shopping, check three things separately: **where the unit sits, what moves around it, and how you will reach its contents**. A rectangle that fits on the floor answers only the first question. This guide helps you compare a small cart or shelf with your existing room; it does not establish building-code or accessibility clearances.

## Start with the items, not the gap

Choose one small job for the storage: two spare hand towels and the toiletries you use at the sink, for example. Set those items together on a dry surface. Put bulk refills and occasional extras aside before deciding how much furniture you need.

Measure your tallest bottle, including its pump, and the folded towel stack. Record the space needed to lift each item out, not just its standing height. A bottle can fit between shelves but still be awkward to remove past a raised edge. If you plan to add a basket, measure that basket too; a shelf's outside width is not its usable inside width.

Keep this trial focused on ordinary towels and toiletries. Medicines, cleaning chemicals, and electrical appliances have separate storage and use requirements; an open cart is not automatically a suitable place for them.

## Measure the narrowest usable space

Sketch the proposed location and name the directions in your own room: **along the wall**, **out into the room**, and **up from the floor**. This prevents a retailer's width and depth labels from getting swapped when you imagine turning the cart.

Measure at the floor, above the baseboard, and near the intended top of the unit. Include projecting trim, a countertop overhang, towel bars, handles, and any other fixed obstacle. Write down the smallest relevant measurement in each direction. Do not count space occupied by pipes or a shutoff valve as empty storage space.

Look for dampness or an active drip before putting anything there. [EPA's moisture guidance](https://www.epa.gov/mold/brief-guide-mold-moisture-and-your-home) emphasizes fixing water problems and drying affected items. Do not hide a wet patch behind a cart. In a rental, report a suspected leak through the appropriate maintenance process before continuing.

For a location inside the vanity rather than beside it, use our [under-sink storage plan](/articles/under-sink-storage-around-pipes/), which maps plumbing and keeps service access visible.

## Read assembled dimensions, not shipping dimensions

Find the exact model's assembled measurements and assembly instructions. Check whether the stated overall size includes handles, feet, and wheels. Ask the manufacturer about an unclear dimension rather than assuming a product photo is to scale.

For a concrete example, [IKEA's U.S. VESKEN cart page](https://www.ikea.com/us/en/p/vesken-cart-white-00471222/) lists an assembled size of 21 1/4 inches wide, 7 1/8 inches deep, and 28 inches high, with packaging measurements in a separate section. Those assembled dimensions describe that particular model, not every slim bathroom cart. The page also supplies its own care directions and load limit. Recheck the exact version before ordering; this is a reading example, not a recommendation or a tested fit.

A product's total height also leaves out the things you put on its top shelf. Add your actual bottle or towel height where it projects above the unit. A low cart with a tall pump bottle can still interfere with a drawer or towel bar.

## Test the footprint and the moving parts

Cut a paper rectangle to the candidate's full outside footprint, including any projecting handle. Place it on the dry floor only while checking, keep it flat, and remove it immediately afterward so it does not become a slip hazard. Do not tape it to an unfamiliar floor finish. If floor testing is impractical, draw the room and footprint to scale instead.

With the paper in place:

1. Open and close the bathroom door through its normal full movement. Watch the bottom edge and handle, not only the door's closed position.
2. Open each vanity door and drawer that comes toward the proposed storage. Check them one at a time, then in combinations your household actually uses.
3. Check access to the sink, toilet, tub or shower, towel storage, and the route out of the room. A spot needed for standing or transferring is not spare storage space.
4. If a cart must pull out, mark its pulled-out position on your sketch. Check where you would stand and where the cart would go while someone opens the door.

Paper has no height. Measure from the floor to any collision point and compare it with the unit **and its contents**. Do not treat passing a floor check as a stability, accessibility, or installation assessment. Preserve any required clearances and the space each household member needs; when those requirements are uncertain, leave the area open and get appropriate guidance.

## Example: a cart that fits the gap but fails the room

Imagine a bathroom with a measured usable gap 10 inches across and 23 inches along the vanity. A hypothetical cart is 9 1/2 inches across, 22 inches long, and 28 inches high. These are invented planning dimensions, not a real product specification or a recommended clearance.

Subtracting the cart from the gap leaves 1/2 inch across and 1 inch along its length in total. That does not mean those amounts are adequate for assembly, movement, cleaning, or the maker's requirements. It only means the two rectangles do not overlap on the sketch.

The paper check reveals the actual problem: the bathroom door sweeps across the front 2 inches of the cart's proposed footprint. The household rejects that location. Wheels do not solve a collision if the unit must be moved every time someone enters.

Instead of buying a smaller cart immediately, they test a handled caddy on an existing vanity shelf for the daily toiletries. Spare towels stay in an existing closet. If those items are reachable and the vanity still works normally, the household may not need another piece of furniture at all. ## Copy this fit worksheet

Use one row per check, and keep unanswered questions visible. A blank specification is a reason to investigate, not a pass.

| Check | Your room or items | Candidate product | Decision or next action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Across the gap | Smallest usable measurement | Full outside size in this direction | Compare without rounding away a conflict |
| Along the wall | Available length after obstacles | Full outside length, including handle | Confirm orientation |
| Height | Lowest relevant obstacle | Unit plus contents above top shelf | Check drawers, bars, and overhangs |
| Door movement | Full sweep and normal opening | Footprint and height in the sweep | Reject a collision |
| Shelf access | Tallest bottle, towel stack, hand access | Usable shelf opening and edge height | Confirm you can remove items |
| Pull-out position | Available route and standing space | Unit's position when in use | Keep fixtures and exit usable |
| Cleaning access | Floor and wall you need to reach | How the unit moves or lifts out | Check instructions and practical access |
| Installation and care | Surface, moisture, household needs | Exact instructions and limits | Resolve every requirement before use |

Save the exact instructions with your measurement notes. Our [home product manual guide](/articles/organize-home-product-manuals/) offers a simple way to find them again.

## Check installation before calling it renter-friendly

“Freestanding” does not mean a piece of furniture can safely skip required anchoring. [CPSC's Anchor It! guidance](https://www.cpsc.gov/Safety-Education/Safety-Education-Centers/AnchorItgov) calls for securing top-heavy furniture and following manufacturer instructions. Review the exact unit's requirements before purchase. Do not replace required anti-tip hardware with an improvised adhesive attachment or assume a smaller-looking unit is safe for climbing children.

If the chosen furniture requires an attachment your rental does not currently allow, resolve that through the [rental update permission process](/articles/ask-landlord-permission-rental-update/) or select a different solution that can be installed as directed. Do not omit the safety step to keep a project reversible.

Follow product-specific cleaning, loading, and placement directions. Keep access to plumbing and ventilation unobstructed, and leave the floor inspectable. Reject a unit that only works by trapping it permanently between fixtures.

The purchase decision is ready when you can identify what the unit holds, show where it sits, open the room's moving parts, retrieve the contents, and meet its installation requirements. If one of those checks fails, reduce the storage job or use existing space before adding furniture.
